Fight IQ

Feet staggered, left forward. Weight even. Hold—no fidgeting.

Bad stance and you're hittable before you throw.
You're buying range with position, not speed.

Right foot leads. Same length, same guard—don't square the chest.

They have to remap angles and leads mid-fight.
Outside foot isn't style—it's who owns the line.

Hands at the jaw. Elbows covering ribs. Chin down. Stay still.

Flared elbows leave the jaw on a rail.
This is what makes their first shot cost something.

Lead arm frames the face. Rear hand high. Nothing drifting forward.

A lazy lead side is a free lane for their rear hand.
Structure beats hoping their straight misses.

Extend the lead straight, full return. No telegraph, no lean.

If something keeps snapping their face, they can't set their feet.
You buy the half-beat for everything else.

Rear fist in one line: back heel, hip, shoulder through.

Arm-only crosses land; body crosses move people.
That's how you punish someone still squared in front of you.

Elbow at height, short arc. Turn the core—don't loop wide.

Straights own the middle; hooks eat the blind side.
They shell forward and forget what's beside them.

Shift weight in, hook tight. Same level in, same level out.

When they're leaning or turning away, the rear hook is there.
Wide swings feed counters—short ones don't.

Drop the level slightly, drive up the channel between the arms.

Chin floats when they're tired or stuck high.
Elbows wide opens the middle—meet it from below.

Load the back leg, punch vertical through the pocket.

Same gap as the lead—more mass behind it.
That's the shot when they're dipping square onto you.

Waist left, chin off centre. Small shift—keep your eyes on them.

You didn't block—you left the address they punched.
Their line is empty; your feet are still under you.

Clear right off the jab lane. Minimal—don't dive.

What they aimed down the middle clips nothing.
You stay balanced—counter's already loaded.

Roll under the arc, then reset—U-shape, not a collapse.

High guard doesn't delete hooks; changing level does.
You come up where their next punch isn't set.

Slip into the roll—one motion, back to stance.

One move beats one punch; the chain beats bursts.
You're gone before the second shot of the combo tracks.

Deflect the wrist offline—tap and angle, don't paw or chase.

You bent their line; they're extended, you aren't.
That inch is the difference between eating and answering.
